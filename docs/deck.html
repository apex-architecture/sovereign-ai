This deck sets that number, and every figure on this page is read out of the module, the command line and the delivered files that produce it — pick a shape and see the pixels it commits to.

Every shape on the deck

A diffusion engine cannot be asked for the delivered size. Its latent grid is downsampled by 8 and then patched by 2, so every side must divide by 16 — which is why CinemaScope is asked for as 832x352 (2.364:1) and delivered as 1920x804 (2.388:1). The name is the intention; the last two columns are the numbers. Nothing here is rounded up to sound exact.

What the deck drives, and what it does not

Set on the deck

  • Shape — the delivery raster, the latent size and one weighted framing clause.
  • Aperture — one weighted depth-of-field clause. It cannot add a lens that is not there.

Chosen somewhere else

The focal length is picked per shot in the shot list, because a scene moves through glass. The compiler reads it from the framing, so a wide establishing shot asks for 24mm and a close-up asks for 85mm without the deck deciding for the director.

What this page is not: a rendered film. The viewfinder shows the proportions of the shape, drawn from the same plate the two proof frames were made from — it is not a preview of what a model will generate. The deck changes the raster and the words; it does not make an engine understand anamorphic flare, and no lens, matte box or shutter is simulated here.
