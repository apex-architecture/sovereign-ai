Operator Console

This is a read-only status board, and it is the first thing worth saying about it: this page starts no job, runs no driver, holds no live cost meter and opens no queue. It cannot, because it is a static file served to a browser — a button here could only animate, and an animation of work in progress would be a lie with a spinner. So there is none. Every figure below is counted from the registry snapshot this site loads, in your browser, as it stands today.

A snapshot, not a control panel. The live operator tooling — the thing that actually presses buttons, spends machine minutes and moves money — runs on the owner's own machine and its own deployment, not here. Where this board uses the word "gate", it means a condition that is either met or not met, plus one physical next action. There are no readiness scores on this page and no percentage of "done", because this platform is either wired or it is not, and the honest answer about most of it is "not yet".

Production plan and its gates

Four waves, quoted from the project's own blueprint. Every gate on this page is binary — met or not met — and every gate carries exactly one next physical action for the day it fails. Nothing here is a measurement taken by this site: the numbers in this block are plan targets written as words, labelled as such.

"What exists at its end" is the deliverable you can hold. "Gate" is the one question that decides whether the wave is over. "If it fails" is the next thing a person physically does.
Wave What exists at its end The gate — pass or fail, nothing in between If it fails: one next action

Wave 1 ceiling, shown as the division it is

Plan target, not a measurement: no more than 90.0 machine-minutes per finished minute of delivered film. The arithmetic that must be shown on every run, numerator over denominator:

measured wall-clock machine-minutes ÷ finished minutes on the delivered piece ≤ 90.0

Wall-clock means the clock on the wall while the machine worked — not an estimate of effort, not a per-task figure anyone typed in. Machine time is measured, never believed: a run that cannot show the two numbers it divided has no business quoting a third. This page shows neither number, because this page owns no clock and runs nothing.

Wave 3 cost floor, shown as the arithmetic it is

Plan target, not a measurement: the price floor under a paid anchor is quoted as numerator times price divided by finished minutes —

20 jobs × $0.65 = $13.00 ÷ 4 finished minutes = $3.25 per finished minute

    The one revenue channel, and the thing that is never the lock

    Verification is priced at zero, forever, and says so on every page of this site: an open receipt must be checkable by anyone holding the file, with no account and no payment, or the guarantee it is checking is worth nothing. The single channel that earns money is a paid anchor on finished work — the act of putting a completed piece onto the record. A creator who never pays can still be verified, and a recipient who never pays can still check.

    Which means the free door can never be the lock on the paid one. If verification ever became the thing you had to buy, the platform would have sold the guarantee rather than the service.

    What a seal is, and what it is not

    A seal is an audit trail: which catalogue row, which inputs, which driver, which file, at what moment, signed by whom. It is not a certificate about reality and this platform never claims to be able to issue one — a seal over a render proves the render came from that run, not that the footage is real, not that the licence behind the tool was read, not that anyone agreed the film is good. The audit trail is the product. The claim about reality is not one this platform is entitled to make.

    Decisions the owner keeps

    These belong to a named human and to no assistant, no automated run and no future agent working on this repository. They are written as roles and categories rather than as internal codes, because the list is about who decides, not about how the plumbing is arranged.
