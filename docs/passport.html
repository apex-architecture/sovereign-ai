Creator Passport

A passport is the bundle a creator owns about a finished work: who made it, which tools touched it, which licence state each tool carried at the moment of use, what each stage actually produced, and the evidence trail that lets a stranger check the work without trusting this platform. Its founding purpose is portability — the delivery package must be readable with zero of our servers running, and it must survive the platform going away. One sentence of state, first: no passport has been issued, because no film has been finished on this platform yet. What follows is the shape, the doctrine, and the measured facts behind it — not a live credential system.

What a passport carries

Every field is marked honestly: specified and measured means the data exists in this snapshot today; specified, not yet issued means the field has a shape in the schema and nothing fills it, because no work has been finished. A chip here describes the schema's state, not the quality of any film.

What a passport never carries

The key rule, stated as design

A passport carries a public identity and evidence. It never carries a private key or any secret. This is not a storage decision to revisit later — a field that could hold a secret does not exist in the schema, so a leaked passport bundle cannot compromise anything, by construction.

What a seal is, and is not

A seal is an audit trail. It proves that a statement was made at a moment; it does not prove the content is true, authorised, or good. Any reader of a passport — including a stranger — should treat every field inside it as a recorded claim, and the passport's value as the trail that lets them check those claims elsewhere.

Compatibility, not conformance

Where field names echo C2PA vocabulary, that is an intention to speak a shared language, not a claim of conformance. Nothing on this page asserts that a standard, a blockchain, or any court guarantees anything about a passport.

Portability test

The acceptance bar the platform holds itself to

UNTESTED

A delivered package — files plus passport — must verify on a second machine with zero of our servers running. Not “mostly offline”. Not “offline with a fallback call to us”. If the proof of the work requires us to be online, the work is not portable and the delivery is a fail, whatever the files look like.

Current gate result: UNTESTED — because no film has been finished, no package exists to hand to a second machine, and the test cannot be run against an absence. This is a binary state, deliberately: the gate has a result or it does not. There is no score, and none will be printed while nothing has been delivered.

Why a creator would want one

A stranger can check the work without an account

The passport is evidence plus a pointer, not an invitation. A client, a festival, or a person who found the film on a memory stick can read who touched which stage, at what depth, under what recorded licence state — without signing up to anything, including us.

The record leaves with the creator

It is a bundle of files the creator owns. If this platform disappears tomorrow, the passport for a finished work is still readable, and the hashes it carries still match the files sitting next to it. Portability is the feature; the platform is incidental.

Tool and licence state travel with each stage

Not a single vague “AI-assisted” label over the whole film: which driver touched which stage, at which depth, and what the licence record said at the moment of use — including “unread”, recorded as itself. That is the level of detail a buyer or a regulator asks for, answered before they ask.
