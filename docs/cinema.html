Act II · The army

Not a metaphor.
A headcount.

Every point of gold above you is one tool we found, named, and wrote down. It is not decoration — the field is drawn straight from the catalogue, so the shape of this sky is the shape of our list.

What this sky is not: a promise. Being on the list means we know the tool exists and what it is for. It does not mean we can drive it, and it does not mean anyone has read its licence.

Act III · The rail

Ten beats,
board to release.

A film walks the same road every time. This is ours — with what actually runs each beat, and where a person still has to sit down and finish the job.

Act IV · The proof

Nobody has to
take your word.

When a film leaves this house it carries a receipt. A stranger with no account, no key and no money can check it — on their own machine, with ours switched off.

And the limit, said out loud: a receipt proves a statement was made about exact bytes at a moment. It never proves the film is true, or good, or that anyone authorised it. A seal is an audit trail, not a verdict.
